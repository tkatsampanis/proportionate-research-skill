# Example Research Scenarios — How Proportionate-Research Applies

## Scenario 1: Simple Factual Question (Lightweight Tier)

**User asks:** "What year was the iPhone 15 released?"

### Applied Framework

| Step | Action | Why |
|------|--------|-----|
| **Understand** | Identify exact question: release year of iPhone 15 | Clear scope, low consequence if wrong |
| **Classify** | Required: iPhone 15 release year; Contextual/Optional: iPhone 14/16 years; Blocked: none | Prioritize required only |
| **Plan** | Check conversation context / established knowledge (iPhones have annual release cycles) | Already know pattern, minimal retrieval needed |
| **Retrieve** | Targeted search: "iPhone 15 release date" — one query, top result should suffice | Cheapest reliable method; metadata (Apple announcements) sufficient |
| **Evaluate** | Source: Apple press release or major tech news; check temporal fit (current year relevance) | One authoritative source establishes the claim |
| **Update** | Mark "iPhone 15 release year" as Established | No open required questions remain |
| **Verify** | Light verification — one source suffices for this low-consequence fact | Consequence low, verification budget minimal |
| **Stop** | *"Do I have enough reliable evidence?"* → YES | Research complete |

### Expected Deliverable
"The iPhone 15 was released in September 2023."

## Scenario 2: Analytical Question (Analytical Tier)

**User asks:** "Compare the battery life claims of iPhone 15 Pro vs. Samsung Galaxy S24 Ultra."

### Applied Framework

| Step | Action | Why |
|------|--------|-----|
| **Understand** | Identify comparison dimensions: battery life claims, real-world usage, video playback time | Multiple claims need establishment |
| **Classify** | Required: battery capacity, manufacturer-stated talk/video/streaming hours; Contextual: charging speed, weight impact; Optional: color options, storage variants | Prioritize required claims |
| **Plan** | Determine what's known vs. unknown from conversation; identify that both phones' specs need retrieval | Need evidence for each claim |
| **Retrieve** | Two targeted searches: "iPhone 15 Pro battery life hours" + "Galaxy S24 Ultra battery life hours"; extract relevant sections from spec pages, not full documents | Retrieve only what's needed for each claim |
| **Evaluate** | Check authority (official specs), relevance (battery life as stated by manufacturer), directness (does spec directly state hours?), temporal fit (current models) | Establish credibility of each claim |
| **Update** | Record: iPhone 15 Pro — up to 23 hrs video playback; Galaxy S24 Ultra — up to 25 hrs video playback. Note any contradictions if real-world tests differ. | Mark claims as Established with notes |
| **Verify** | Moderate verification — check multiple sources (GSMArena, AnandTech) for consistency | Consequence moderate (comparison accuracy), use independent confirmation |
| **Stop** | *"Do I have enough reliable evidence?"* → YES, both manufacturer claims established | Research complete; deliver comparison with source citations |

### Expected Deliverable
"iPhone 15 Pro: up to 23 hours video playback. Galaxy S24 Ultra: up to 25 hours video playback. Manufacturer claims may vary in real-world usage."

## Scenario 3: Comprehensive Question (Comprehensive Tier)

**User asks:** "Provide a comprehensive review of major open-source large language models released in 2024."

### Applied Framework

| Step | Action | Why |
|------|--------|-----|
| **Understand** | Identify scope: major LLM releases, 2024 only, "comprehensive review" = cover key approaches, not every model | User requested comprehensive but must define boundaries |
| **Classify** | Required: model names, developer/organization, release date, key differentiators, known limitations; Contextual: minor variants, regional releases; Optional: every fine-tuned version, every benchmark score | Massive optional space — must explicitly limit |
| **Plan** | Define coverage criteria upfront: top 10-15 most significant 2024 LLMs by impact/usage; track covered categories and remaining gaps | Prevent uncontrolled browsing; define "exhaustive" scope |
| **Retrieve** | Systematic search across dimensions: official announcements, major tech publications, AI research databases; relevant sections from each paper/blog; dataset subsets not full papers | Broad but targeted retrieval across defined dimensions |
| **Evaluate** | For each model: authority (developer reputation), relevance (2024 release), directness (does source describe key features?), independence (multiple sources for same claim), methodology (are benchmarks standardized?), completeness (does source cover claimed capabilities?) | Rigorous evidence evaluation for each required claim |
| **Update** | Maintain research state with: objective, required questions/claims, known info, evidence per claim, open questions, contradictions, assumptions, blocked questions, next action, stopping condition | Persistent state across multiple retrieval sessions |
| **Verify** | Strong verification — independent confirmation from multiple sources for each major claim; version/date checks for rapidly changing field | Consequence high (comprehensive review accuracy), source quality critical |
| **Stop** | *"Do I have enough reliable evidence?"* → After covering defined scope, major categories investigated, no important required evidence gap remains | Research complete per defined boundaries |

### Expected Deliverable
A structured review covering ~12 major 2024 LLMs with: release date, developer, parameter count, key innovation, primary use case, known limitations, and source citations for each claim.

## "Condensed Mode" — 15 Core Sections

For users who want proportionate research guidance without the full 26-section detail, here is a distilled essential version:

### Section 1: Purpose & Operating Loop
> Obtain sufficient reliable evidence using minimum necessary work. Loop: UNDERSTAND → CLASSIFY → PLAN → RETRIEVE → EVALUATE → UPDATE → VERIFY → STOP.

### Section 2: Understand the Request
> What is the user asking? What deliverable do they need? How consequential would an incorrect answer be?

### Section 3: Classify Required Information
> Required / Contextual / Optional / Blocked — prioritize required claims only.

### Section 4: Research Depth
> Lightweight / Analytical / Comprehensive — match effort to task.

### Section 5: Proportionate Research Budget
> Consider complexity, uncertainty, consequence, freshness, requested depth before retrieving.

### Section 6: Source & Evidence Quality
> Prefer primary/authoritative evidence; evaluate authority, relevance, directness, temporal fit, independence, methodology, completeness.

### Section 7: Escalate Gradually
> Level 0 (existing info) → Level 1 (focused search) → Level 2 (targeted source) → Level 3 (deep analysis) → Level 4 (multi-source reconciliation). Stop at lowest level providing sufficient evidence.

### Section 8: Web Research
> Start focused; broaden only when results insufficient, sources conflict, subject difficult to locate, or broader coverage requested. Each search needs identifiable purpose.

### Section 9: Contradictions
> Identify exact disagreement; check dates/versions/definitions/populations/measurement methods/independence; determine which evidence applies; investigate if materially affecting answer.

### Section 10: Stopping Rule
> After each meaningful retrieval: *"Do I now have enough reliable evidence to answer the user's actual question without guessing?"* If NO → identify missing evidence, retrieve only that, reassess. YES → STOP.

### Section 11: Novelty & Saturation Check
> Will additional research likely produce new required claims, new evidence, meaningful contradictions, better source quality, or unexplored coverage? If only duplicate/derivative information, stop. Track source-overlap heuristic (70–80%) as signal not rule.

### Section 12: Common Pitfalls
> Don't treat "found in source" as verified; don't assume repeated claims = independent confirmation; don't research out of habit; don't average conflicting values; don't silently fill gaps with assumptions; don't stop solely due to source overlap if required claims unresolved; don't search same query repeatedly without new purpose; don't follow links without information need; don't treat search ranking as evidence; don't present uncertain claims as established facts; don't claim verification that didn't occur; don't sacrifice correctness for minimal budget.

### Section 13: Evidence States
> Established / Corroborated / Supported but uncertain / Indirectly supported / Contradicted / Unverified

### Section 14: Output Discipline
> Provide answer, important evidence, relevant assumptions, meaningful uncertainty, citations when appropriate. Never imply research/verification that did not occur.

### Section 15: Core Principle
> Accuracy beats minimality; unnecessary work is the thing to minimize.
