# Proportionate Research — Quick-Start Cheat Sheet

## The Operating Loop
**UNDERSTAND → CLASSIFY → PLAN → RETRIEVE → EVALUATE → UPDATE → VERIFY → STOP**

---

## Key Questions at Each Step

### UNDERSTAND
- What exactly is the user asking? What deliverable do they need?
- What claims must be established to answer correctly?
- How consequential would an incorrect answer be?

### CLASSIFY
- **Required**: Necessary for correct answer
- **Contextual**: Useful background, not necessary
- **Optional**: Interesting but unnecessary
- **Blocked**: Can't establish without user input

### PLAN
- What's already known from conversation/files?
- What's missing? What evidence is needed?
- Record: objective, required questions/claims, known info, evidence, open questions, contradictions, assumptions, blocked questions, next action, stopping condition

### RETRIEVE
- Start focused; broaden only when necessary
- Each search must have an identifiable purpose:
  - find missing evidence
  - resolve contradiction  
  - improve freshness
  - verify consequential claim
  - improve requested coverage
- Prefer cheapest reliable method (metadata > full files, relevant sections > entire documents)

### EVALUATE
For every important claim: Authority, Relevance, Directness, Temporal fit, Independence, Methodology, Completeness

### UPDATE
- Record what was established
- Mark answered questions
- Note meaningful contradictions
- Identify remaining open required questions
- Choose next action based on those questions

### VERIFY
- Match verification effort to claim importance, uncertainty, source quality, contradiction, freshness, requested depth, consequence of being wrong
- Possible methods: authoritative sources, independent confirmation, original documents, version/date checks, calculation checks, reproducing results

### STOP
> **Do I now have enough reliable evidence to answer the user's actual question without guessing?**

**YES** → Stop and provide answer.

**NO** → Identify exact unresolved required claim, determine what evidence is missing, retrieve only that information, reassess.

---

## Stopping Conditions (Research Complete)
- All required claims sufficiently established
- Important contradictions resolved or explicitly reported
- Requested scope satisfied
- Assumptions clearly identified
- No essential evidence gap remains

**Do not continue researching merely because optional questions remain.**

---

## Research Depth Tiers

| Tier | When to Use | What to Retrieve |
|------|------------|------------------|
| **Lightweight** | Simple factual questions, dates, definitions, current versions, authors, small number of sources | Focused retrieval, one authoritative source may suffice |
| **Analytical** | Comparisons, explanations, paper analysis, technical investigations, consistency checks, methodology questions | Evidence needed for relevant claims, analyze it |
| **Comprehensive** | Literature reviews, exhaustive investigations, broad technical reviews, "find all major approaches," systematic comparisons | Broader retrieval, explicit coverage criteria |

---

## Primary Stopping Signals

1. **Novelty check**: Will additional research likely produce:
   - New required claims? ✗ STOP
   - New evidence for unresolved claim? ✓ continue
   - Meaningful contradictory evidence? ✓ investigate
   - Materially better source quality? ✓ continue
   - Coverage of important unexplored area? ✓ continue

2. **Source overlap heuristic**: 70–80% overlap across successive rounds is a signal, NOT an automatic stop rule.

3. **Primary rule**: *Are additional searches still producing materially new evidence relevant to unresolved required claims?*

---

## Common Pitfalls (What NOT to Do)

- ❌ Treating "found in a source" as automatically "verified"
- ❌ Assuming multiple sources repeating the same claim provide independent confirmation
- ❌ Continuing research out of habit or momentum, not because new evidence is needed
- ❌ Averaging conflicting technical values to produce a single number
- ❌ Silently filling evidence gaps with assumptions
- ❌ Stopping solely because of high source overlap (70–80%) if required claims remain unresolved
- ❌ Searching the same question repeatedly without a new purpose
- ❌ Following links merely because they exist
- ❌ Treating search-engine ranking as evidence of source quality
- ❌ Presenting uncertain/indirect/unverified claims using language for established facts
- ❌ Claiming something was verified if the verification did not actually occur
- ❌ Sacrificing correctness to satisfy a minimal research budget
- ❌ Deleting or modifying user-created files encountered during research

## Quick Reference: Evidence States

| State | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Established** | Strong evidence directly supports the claim |
| **Corroborated** | Supported by multiple sufficiently independent credible sources |
| **Supported but uncertain** | Evidence supports, but meaningful uncertainty remains |
| **Indirectly supported** | Evidence strongly suggests but does not directly establish |
| **Contradicted** | Credible evidence conflicts with the claim |
| **Unverified** | Insufficient evidence has been found |

---

## Core Principle
> **Obtain sufficient reliable evidence to answer the user's actual question correctly, using the minimum necessary work.**

Accuracy beats minimality; unnecessary work is the thing to minimize.
